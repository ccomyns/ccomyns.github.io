Since high school and far before then, I’ve felt a huge sense of indebtedness to those that came before me. If growth hormones didn’t exist, my full grown height would’ve been 4’11”. If the internet didn’t exist, I would’ve lost touch with my middle and high school friends. The list goes on and on, but with every generation of technological breakthroughs, problems that people dealt with in the past go away. That’s not to say that people don’t experience hardships and that innovations don’t introduce new problems. They do. But problems that were once unfixable become fixable. 

For those that know me, thinking about the future and *what could be* isn’t just a pastime, it’s a passion. It’s why I joined a computational biology research group my sophomore year of high school and a tRNA engineering group the following year. I want to discover new things, and just like the scientists before me, I want to leave the world a better place than I found it. 

Even though I did enjoy biology research, I pivoted away from it because the pace of discovery was too slow. I wanted to find a field of work where my efforts could have an outsized impact on society, and unsurprisingly, I landed on AI.

AI is the ultimate leverage tool, but harnessing that leverage is incredibly difficult. What type of mental model should you have for something that’s capable of PhD-level math but can’t count the number of r’s in strawberry? I don’t have a concrete answer to this, but I point to a recent example that has been influential in my own thinking:

**AI Scientist-v2**: in April 2025, a research group called Sakana AI released an automated research pipeline, which begins with a research topic and spits out a full publication. To be a little more specific, this pipeline has 6 stages: (1) it ingests and processes hundreds of publications (2) it makes a research plan (3) it writes thousands of lines of experimental code (4) it runs this code on GPUs (5) it interprets the results of these experiments (6) it then constructs a publication with everything from the previous stages. From this simple pipeline (which relied on Sonnet 3.5 at the time and has minimal scaffolding), Sakana AI was able to create a fully autonomous publication that passed multiple peer reviews and was selected to be a conference paper at ICLR (one of the most competitive AI research conferences with a ~30% acceptance rate).

I listed this example in particular because I think it contradicts many peoples’ mental models of AI. Yes, AI is truly capable of novel discoveries, and no it is not just a massive plagiarism machine. AI will usher us into a world of radical abundance. It's just a matter of time. It may not come tomorrow or even within the next 20 years, but it is truly an exciting time to be alive
